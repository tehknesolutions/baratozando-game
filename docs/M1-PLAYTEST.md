# M1 Playtest — First Threat

## Goal

Validate the first horror/suspense beat: the player should understand “RUN” without a tutorial paragraph and feel pressure without the chase becoming arbitrary.

## Checklist

- [ ] The calm opening gives enough time to reacquire M0 controls.
- [ ] The pre-chase checkpoint activates before the trigger.
- [ ] Crossing the trigger produces an unmistakable dramatic reaction.
- [ ] `CORRA.` appears at the correct moment and does not obstruct platform reading.
- [ ] The Ancient Predator reads as dramatically larger than the player.
- [ ] Walking after the warning feels unsafe.
- [ ] Running plus clean jumps is enough to escape without perfect play.
- [ ] Dodge is useful but not mandatory for every successful attempt.
- [ ] The predator visually contacts the player at roughly the same moment the catch occurs.
- [ ] Catch produces one death event, not repeated HP/death spam.
- [ ] Respawn returns to the pre-chase checkpoint.
- [ ] Predator resets fully after respawn.
- [ ] A second chase attempt works exactly like the first.
- [ ] Falling out of the room resets both player and chase.
- [ ] Reaching the safe line wins even if the predator is very close.
- [ ] `BARATOZOU.` feels satisfying rather than comedic enough to destroy the horror tone.

## Tuning questions

After 3–5 attempts:

1. Is 650 ms warning too short, right, or too long?
2. Does 205 px/s create tension, or is the threat too forgiving?
3. Is the 1,235 px route long enough to build drama without repetition?
4. Do camera shake and red tint increase suspense without hurting readability?
5. Does the silhouette feel like an “Ancient Predator” even before final art/audio?

M2 should not begin until catch timing, threat speed and presentation are tuned from this playtest.
